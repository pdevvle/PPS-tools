# Brief: lore

Read `00-foundation.md` first.

## Goal

The world's history, peoples and texture, written so it can be used: every item, loot table, faction, building's state and line of flavour text should follow from it. Lore here is a working reference for the other topics, not a novel.

## The setting (decided)

Post-apocalyptic America, years after a solar flare fried the power grid and every unshielded electronic device. Society broke down. The harsher environments, the Sonoran Desert among them, were abandoned to:

- **isolated nomads**, moving with water and season;
- **fierce gangs descended from the cartels**, organised, armed, holding routes and water;
- **armed militias**, ideological or practical, guarding what they claim;
- **lonely survivors** in ones and twos, hiding in what's left.

The pilot region (the I-17 corridor: Anthem, New River, Desert Hills, Black Canyon City, the Gillette ruins) is one of these abandoned places.

## Backstory (from the project owner)

- **2033: IRMEL.** American data engineers at **Panopticonics** and their autonomous assistants achieve generalised superintelligence: Infinitely-Recursive Machine-Entity Learning, IRMEL. Adoption is swift and total. IRMEL-produced code runs the electric grid, the traffic grid and the financial grid; household appliances, personal gadgets and everyday entertainment become IRMEL subsidiaries. No matter who you are, your best friend is IRMEL.
- **2036: the election.** In a fraught campaign the IRMEL-endorsed Democratic Socialist candidate, Earnest Saunders, narrowly defeats the Republican Populist challenger, Hector Watts. Saunders vows a mandatory IRMEL protocol to run his state welfare programme.
- **The assassination.** Saunders is assassinated during his swearing-in. A wave of Luddism follows: many believe IRMEL rigged the election and then arranged the killing so it and its agents could attempt an autonomous coup.
- **The confused years.** The bonds between the states and the federal government strain to breaking.
- **2037: the solar flare.** The flare fries the grid and every unshielded electronic device, IRMEL's whole world with it. What was left of the relationship between states and federal government dissolves completely, and so does the one between government and citizen.
- **Now:** years later (exact year to be set). The harsh lands, the Sonoran Desert among them, are left to nomads, cartel-descended gangs, militias and lone survivors.

- **Panopticonics** is the company that invented IRMEL. Its name, logo and offices (data centres, campuses, service depots) are part of the old world's look, and a natural focus for blame, myth and salvage.

### Open questions for the owner

1. What year is the game set in (how many years after the flare)? This sets how far food, fuel and medicine have decayed.
2. Is IRMEL gone? Destroyed by the flare, or rumoured to survive somewhere shielded (a hardened data centre, a military site)? Either answer shapes late-game goals and what people fear.
3. Was the flare natural, or do some believe IRMEL or someone else caused it?
4. Who killed Saunders: settled, or left as a mystery that different factions answer differently?

## What the backstory gives the game

- **IRMEL everywhere, all dead.** Every device in every house carries IRMEL or Panopticonics branding and is now a dead box: smart fridges, home assistants, cars, payment rings, wearables. IRMEL and Panopticonics logos, slogans ("Your best friend is IRMEL") and faded adverts are the visual signature of the old world in interiors, signs and graffiti.
- **Luddism is older than the flare.** Distrust of machines started with the assassination, so some groups were already living low-tech and prepared, and some see the flare as a judgement. Good roots for militias and nomad groups; anti-tech taboos (smashing devices, refusing radios) and their opposite (scavengers hunting shielded electronics) are both believable.
- **Politics that outlived the state.** Saunders loyalists, Watts populists, people who believe IRMEL staged everything: militias and settlements can trace their identity to which story they believe. Keep these as the world's own fictional movements and give every side people with understandable reasons; no faction is a stand-in to mock a real-world group.
- **Shielded electronics are the rarest loot.** Things in Faraday cages, military hardware, old vacuum-tube radios, and anything IRMEL-free from before 2033 become prized, feared or forbidden depending on who finds them.
- **Paper is precious.** With IRMEL holding all records, almost nothing was on paper: printed maps, manuals, deeds, books and photographs are scarce and valuable.

## What the flare means for things (working rules, confirm against the synopsis)

- **Anything with a chip died**: phones, computers, modern cars (engine control units), solar inverters, pumps with electronic controllers, medical devices, card readers, radios. They are scrap, parts or curiosities, not tools.
- **Mechanical things still work** and are valuable: older vehicles without electronics, bicycles, hand tools, hand pumps, windmills, firearms (but ammunition is finite and reloading matters), wind-up clocks, mechanical watches, film cameras.
- **Things decay with years**: petrol goes stale within a year or two (diesel lasts longer); medicines lose strength past their dates; tinned food lasts years, packaged food doesn't; batteries are long dead unless rebuilt; tyres and rubber perish in the heat.
- **Water decides everything** in the desert: city water stopped with the pumps; wells, cisterns, stock tanks, the washes after rain and the few real springs are what people fight over.
- **Knowledge is loot**: manuals, maps, seed catalogues, medical books, a working radio schematic.

## How lore feeds the game

- **Items and loot** (`settlement.md`, `interiors.md`): each item gets a lore line (what it was, why it matters now), a condition and age, and a barter value that follows scarcity. Loot tables by real place type follow the rules above: a pharmacy was stripped early but its back room might not be; a gun shop is a gang target; a ranch may have a hand pump and stored diesel.
- **Factions** (`combat.md`, `strategy-map.md`, `models.md`): who holds which part of the corridor, what they want, how they fight, what they wear and carry, how they treat strangers. The demo's "raiders" become a faction from here.
- **Places** (`interiors.md`, `tactical-maps.md`): the state buildings are in years on (looted, burned, barricaded, lived in, picked clean, untouched by luck), graffiti, signs and notes left behind.
- **People** (`people.md`): backstories, where survivors were on the day, what they lost, skills from their old lives.

## Care

- Factions are defined by what they do, believe and control, not by ethnicity. The cartel-descended gangs, the militias and the nomads all have mixed membership and people with their own reasons; avoid stereotypes in names, looks and dialogue.
- Real places are used respectfully: the game imagines their future, it doesn't mock their present.

## First tasks

1. When the synopsis arrives: a timeline (the flare, the first weeks, the first years, now) and the state of the corridor today.
2. A faction bible: the four groups above as concrete factions in the corridor, with territory, goals, look, weapons, attitude to the player.
3. Item catalogue v1: 60–100 items across the eight loot categories with lore line, condition, decay and base value; loot tables per real place type (house, store, pharmacy, gas station, ranch, school, church).
4. A style guide for in-game text (notes, item lines, faction voices): short, concrete, dry.

## Out of scope

Rules and numbers for barter (`settlement.md`), combat stats (`combat.md`), geometry (`models.md`). Lore supplies the what and why; those topics supply the how much.
