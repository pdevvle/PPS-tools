What makes it read as Zomboid

  • Real human proportions and skin tones instead of plastic dummies.
  • Detail lives in the textures: denim twill, plaid, woodland camo, seams, pockets, belts, zips and buttons.
  • Everyday layered clothing: hoodies, flannel, windbreakers, jeans, beanies, caps and sneakers next to military kit.
  • Painted faces with brows, eyes, stubble and hairlines, and simple hair shells for volume.
  • Wear states: dirt collects low on the legs, and blood splatters the clothes and face.
  • An isometric camera over a tile-painted street, the way Zomboid frames the world.

What it would take in the game

  • The skeleton is the same joint layout the game already animates, so walking, aiming and overwatch poses work unchanged. The patrolling trooper uses the game's walk cycle.
  • Each soldier needs about eight small canvas textures, painted once when the soldier is recruited. That's fast, but more GPU memory than flat colours.
  • Wear could follow the campaign: soldiers get dirtier each turn and bloodied when wounded.
  • Outfits could be generated per recruit, so every rookie looks different.
  • The props would need a matching pass (painted, weathered textures) to sit well next to these characters.
