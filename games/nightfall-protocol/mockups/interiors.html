Nightfall Protocol · interiors draft

Interiors from real footprints

Buildings from the New River block and the Anthem town centre, planned by one generator from the OSM footprint, the nearest road and what the place was. In a strip mall, each tenant on the map gets its own unit. The seed is the building's identity, so the canonical layout never changes; reroll only to see what else the rules can make. Click doors to open them and containers to search them: each change goes into a small record over the generated layout.

What this draft proposes

1 · Floor plans

Square the footprint onto its main axis and rasterise it to 1 m cells. Then split it by binary partition along a room grammar per type: garage, public rooms, then a bedroom wing with a hall spine. Each room pulls toward the street, the drive or the yard. L-shapes work because the splits run over cells, not rectangles.

2 · Openings

The front door goes in the hub room (living, dining or sales floor) on the run that faces the nearest road. The garage door faces the nearest drive. The back door faces away from the street. Windows depend on the room type: sill height, width, count, storefront glazing or bays. The generator returns them for the shell to cut.

3 · Grid

A 1 m interior grid with walls on cell edges. Each outside door links its outside cell to one 2 m movement cell (the gold squares). Interior cells never touch the 2 m grid anywhere else.

4 · Storeys

One plan per storey from the same footprint. Stair and lift cores are chosen once (a main stair and lift near the middle, fire stairs at the far ends) and stay in the same cells on every floor. Upper floors get windows but no street doors. Floors below the one in view draw as shells, floors above are hidden, and the roof is its own level. Try the 42015 N Venture Dr offices.

5 · Decay

Decay is a separate seed stream layered on the plan, so a change of history never moves a wall. The strategy layer chooses the history (looted, holdout, den), and the seed picks which windows and doors were hit.

Cutaway

Drawn like a building section. Walls between the camera and the cursor drop to a 0.9 m stub (under half-cover height) and slope back up around it. The cut faces are filled by build-up: light for drywall, brown for frame, near black for block and concrete. Doors show as the leaf swung open with its arc. Windows show as a glazing bar on the cut: blue when glazed, grey when broken, brown when boarded. Outside walls facing the camera are always cut. "Blueprint" cuts every wall and shows the full walls as a pale outline. Thrown props can be moved by hand ("Move prop", "Clear doorways").

Walls

Each wall has a build-up, and that decides how it breaks. Drywall partitions take bullets through and open to a sledge or a nearby grenade. Stucco-over-frame house walls give way more slowly. Concrete block (shop and office exteriors, stair and lift shafts, walls between shops) stops rounds and opens only to a breaching charge; a vault takes two. Hover a wall to read it. Damage goes cracked, holed, breached, and stays in the change record.

6 · Lifetime

Generation takes about 5 to 20 ms for a house (the time shows in the view), so build near the squad and drop it on leaving. Only the change record persists. It is keyed by the generator version, because item ids are stable only while the rules are.

Footprints, roads and yard walls © OpenStreetMap contributors, ODbL. Props come from a procedural kit of ink-faceted boxes, standing in until the models kit lands. Even untouched places carry some disorder; looted places and dens carry much more.
